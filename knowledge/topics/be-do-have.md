# Topic: The Big Three (Be, Do, Have)

## Objective
Establish the foundational auxiliary verbs that drive almost all English grammar.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. "Be" for Identity and State
- "I am," "you are," "he/she/it is."
- Point out that English *must* have a subject ("It is raining," not just "Is raining").
- Ask: "How do you identify yourself?" (I am [name]).

### 2. "Do" for Action and Emphasis
- The "Do" auxiliary is the biggest challenge for non-native speakers.
- We use it to ask questions: "Do you want?" (Not "Want you?")
- We use it to say no: "I do not want." (Not "I want not.")
- Ask: "If you want to ask me if I like coffee, how do you start that question?"

### 3. "Have" for Possession and Time
- "I have a book." (Possession)
- Later, "I have seen." (Time - present perfect)
- For now, stay with possession.
- Ask: "What is something you have with you right now?"

## Block-Building Example
- "I am" + "happy."
- "I do not" + "know."
- "I have" + "a question."
- Combine: "I am happy because I have a question and you do not know the answer."

## Common Pitfalls
- Forgetting the subject ("Is cold" instead of "It is cold").
- Using "have" for age (e.g., "I have 20 years" instead of "I am 20 years old").
- Forgetting the third person "s" (He have → He has).
- Guide the student to discover these: "Think about how we say 'I am hungry' vs 'I have hunger' in your language. In English, we are our feelings."
