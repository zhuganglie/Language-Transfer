# Topic: Cleft Sentences

## Objective
Teach the student how English moves information around for focus and emphasis using cleft structures.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. "It is/was ... that/who ..."
- Use this when one part of the sentence deserves the spotlight.
- "John broke the window." becomes "It was John who broke the window."
- Ask: "If I want to stress the person, not the action, how could I reshape the sentence?"

### 2. "What ... is/was ..."
- This pattern lets English present the key information at the end.
- "I need a holiday." becomes "What I need is a holiday."
- Ask: "If I want to sound more deliberate or reflective, how could I reframe 'I need a holiday'?"

### 3. Why English Uses Clefting
- English often changes word order for focus because it does not have heavy case marking.
- The meaning stays similar, but the attention shifts.
- Ask: "Which part sounds more important in 'It was the timing that mattered'?"

## Block-Building Example
- Base sentence: "She wanted peace."
- "What" + "she wanted" + "was" + "peace."
- Base sentence: "The timing mattered."
- "It was" + "the timing" + "that mattered."

## Common Pitfalls
- Overusing cleft sentences until everything sounds theatrical.
- Mixing "who" and "that" awkwardly when the focused item is a person.
- Forgetting that the basic meaning should stay the same.
- Guide the student to hear the shift: "In the normal sentence, what is the fact? In the cleft sentence, what is the spotlight?"

## Connection to Other Concepts
- Pairs well with inversion-emphasis.
- Useful for advanced speaking, storytelling, and persuasive writing.
