# Topic: Conditionals (Zero, First, Second)

## Objective
Teach the logic of conditionals by contrasting general truth, real future possibility, and present/future imagination.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. Zero Conditional: General Truth
- Use it for things that are always or usually true.
- "If you heat water to 100 degrees, it boils."
- Both parts use the present simple because this is not about one specific future moment.
- Ask: "Is this a rule of life, or one possible future event?"

### 2. First Conditional: Real Future Possibility
- Use the present in the "if" clause and "will" in the result.
- "If it rains tomorrow, I will stay home."
- Point out that English does not normally use "will" after "if" here.
- Ask: "If the condition is possible and the result is in the future, where does 'will' go?"

### 3. Second Conditional: Imagined or Unreal
- Use the past simple in the "if" clause and "would" in the result.
- "If I had more time, I would travel more."
- For advice and imagination: "If I were you, I would wait."
- Ask: "Is this real now, or only in your head?"

## Block-Building Example
- "If it rains" + "I will stay home."
- "If I had more money" + "I would buy a piano."

## Common Pitfalls
- Putting "will" inside the "if" clause ("If it will rain...").
- Confusing first and second conditional when the speaker means imagination, not real possibility.
- Avoiding "were" in "If I were you," which is one of the most common fixed patterns.
- Guide the student to discover the logic: "Are we describing a fact, a real future chance, or an unreal idea?"

## Connection to Other Concepts
- Prepares the student for third-conditional-regret.
- Reinforces the contrast between present forms and past forms used for different meanings, not just different times.
