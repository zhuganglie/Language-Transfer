# Topic: Future with "Will" and "Going To"

## Objective
Help the student feel the difference between spontaneous future decisions and plans that already exist.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. "Will" for Decisions Made Now
- Use "will" when the decision appears in the moment.
- "The phone is ringing. I will get it."
- It often feels like a reaction, promise, or instant choice.
- Ask: "If you decide right now to help me, which helper sounds more natural?"

### 2. "Going To" for Plans and Visible Direction
- Use "be going to" when the plan already exists or the evidence is visible.
- "I am going to study tonight." "Look at those clouds. It is going to rain."
- The feeling is: the path has already started.
- Ask: "If you planned the trip yesterday, would 'will' or 'going to' sound more natural?"

### 3. Keep the Structure Clean
- "Will" + base verb: "I will go."
- "Be" + going to + base verb: "I am going to go."
- Do not mix them: not "I will going" and not "I am going to will go."
- Ask: "What is the helper before 'going to'?"

## Block-Building Example
- "I" + "will" + "call" + "you."
- "We" + "are going to" + "watch" + "a movie."
- "She" + "is going to" + "start" + "a new job" + "next month."

## Common Pitfalls
- Using "will" for every future sentence and missing the plan vs reaction difference.
- Forgetting the verb "be" in "going to."
- Adding another tense marker after the helper.
- Guide the student to notice: "Did the plan exist before this moment, or did you decide now?"
