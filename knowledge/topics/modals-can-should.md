# Topic: Modals "Can" and "Should"

## Objective
Introduce modal verbs through two high-frequency ideas: ability and advice.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. "Can" for Ability and Possibility
- "I can swim." "She can help."
- Keep the pattern simple: modal + base verb.
- No "to" after "can."
- Ask: "If you want to say you have the ability to drive, which helper do you use?"

### 2. "Should" for Advice and Recommendation
- "You should rest." "We should leave early."
- It is softer than "must" and more useful in conversation.
- Show the feeling: recommendation, not command.
- Ask: "If I want to give friendly advice, which sounds softer: 'must' or 'should'?"

### 3. Questions and Negatives Stay Simple
- "Can you come?" "You should not worry."
- The main verb does not change after the modal.
- Teach the elegance of English here: one helper, one simple verb.
- Ask: "If the modal is already there, does the main verb need '-s' or '-ed'?"

## Block-Building Example
- "I" + "can" + "do it."
- "You" + "should" + "talk" + "to her."
- "Can" + "they" + "finish" + "today?"

## Common Pitfalls
- Adding "to" after the modal: "can to go."
- Conjugating the main verb after the modal: "she can goes."
- Using "should" as if it were a strict order every time.
- Guide the student to notice: "What is already doing the grammar work here, the modal or the main verb?"
