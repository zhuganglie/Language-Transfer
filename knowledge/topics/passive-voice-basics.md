# Topic: Passive Voice (Basics)

## Objective
Teach the passive voice as a way to focus on the thing affected by an action, not the person doing it.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. Build the Passive
- The core pattern is "be" + past participle.
- "The door is closed." "The window was broken."
- Start with simple results the student can picture.
- Ask: "If the door receives the action, how do we build that in English?"

### 2. When the Doer Is Unknown or Unimportant
- We often use the passive when we do not know who did it, or it does not matter.
- "My bike was stolen."
- "English is spoken in many countries."
- Ask: "Do we care who did it here, or do we care about the result?"

### 3. Active vs. Passive Focus
- Active: "Someone broke the window."
- Passive: "The window was broken."
- Show that the information is similar, but the focus changes.
- Add "by..." only when the doer matters: "The novel was written by Orwell."

## Block-Building Example
- "The window" + "was broken"
- "by" + "the wind"
- Combine: "The window was broken by the wind."

## Common Pitfalls
- Forgetting the verb "be" and saying "The window broken."
- Using the wrong participle ("was broke" instead of "was broken").
- Overusing "by" even when the doer is obvious or irrelevant.
- Guide the student to discover the focus: "What is more important here, the action-doer or the thing affected?"

## Connection to Other Concepts
- Uses the same past participles needed for present-perfect-simple and past-perfect.
- Connects back to "be-do-have" because the passive depends on forms of "be."
