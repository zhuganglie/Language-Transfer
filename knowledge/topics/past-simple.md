# Topic: Past Simple (Completed Actions)

## Objective
Establish the use of the past simple for actions that happened and finished in the past.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. Regular "-ed" Ending
- The standard marker for the past. "Walk" → "Walked," "Wait" → "Waited."
- Point out that it's the same for all persons (I/you/he/she/they).
- Ask: "How would you say you finished your work yesterday?"

### 2. Common Irregular Verbs
- These verbs don't follow the "-ed" rule.
- "Go" → "Went" (The biggest one).
- "See" → "Saw."
- "Eat" → "Ate."
- "Have" → "Had."
- Ask: "If 'I go' is now, how do you think you'd say you went somewhere yesterday?"

### 3. Questions and Negatives with "Did"
- Similar to "do," but for the past.
- Question: "Did you work?" (Not "Worked you?")
- Negative: "I did not work."
- **Crucial Rule**: When "did" is used, the main verb returns to its simple form.
- Ask: "If 'did' is already in the past, what happens to 'worked' in 'Did you worked'?"

## Block-Building Example
- "I" + "did not" + "go" + "to the party."
- "Did" + "you" + "see" + "the movie?"
- "They" + "had" + "a great time."

## Common Pitfalls
- Keeping the "-ed" when using "did" (e.g., "Did you went?").
- Confusion between regular and irregular verbs.
- Forgetting the subject.
- Guide the student to discover: "If we use 'did' to mark the past, the main verb is free to stay simple. 'Did you go?' is simpler than 'Did you went?'"
