# Topic: Phrasal Verbs (Introduction)

## Objective
Introduce phrasal verbs as verb + particle combinations where the particle carries real meaning.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. The Particle Is Not Decoration
- "Up," "out," "off," and "on" often add a visual or emotional direction.
- "Stand up," "go out," "turn off," "put on."
- Start with physical meanings before moving to abstract ones.
- Ask: "What picture do you get from the particle?"

### 2. From Literal to Abstract
- Literal: "Pick up the book."
- Less literal: "Pick up a language."
- Abstract: "Give up smoking."
- Show that the particle still hints at the meaning: "up" often suggests completion or intensity.
- Ask: "If something goes 'up,' does it feel more finished, stronger, or higher?"

### 3. Word Order Basics
- Some phrasal verbs can split: "Pick up the book" / "Pick the book up."
- With pronouns, the pronoun usually goes in the middle: "Pick it up."
- Start with a few common, high-frequency examples instead of general rules.

## Block-Building Example
- "I" + "picked" + "it" + "up"
- "She" + "gave" + "up" + "sugar"
- Combine: "I picked it up." / "She gave up sugar."

## Common Pitfalls
- Translating directly from the native language and choosing the wrong particle.
- Treating the particle as optional when it changes the meaning completely.
- Saying "pick up it" instead of "pick it up."
- Guide the student to discover the logic: "What does this particle add to the verb?"

## Connection to Other Concepts
- Builds on prepositions-place-time because many particles begin as simple spatial words.
- Supports conversational fluency and register awareness later.
