# Topic: Prepositions of Place and Time

## Objective
Build an intuitive feel for the high-frequency prepositions "in," "on," and "at."

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. "At" as a Point
- Use "at" for points in space or time: "at the door," "at 5 o'clock."
- It feels small and exact.
- Ask: "If I mean one exact time, like 7:30, which preposition sounds right?"

### 2. "On" as a Surface or Day
- Use "on" for surfaces and for days/dates: "on the table," "on Monday," "on July 3."
- The mental picture helps more than a rule list.
- Ask: "If Monday feels like a specific day you can point to, which preposition fits?"

### 3. "In" as a Container or Larger Period
- Use "in" for enclosed spaces and bigger time containers: "in the room," "in 2026," "in the morning."
- Think of being inside a box, month, year, city, or space.
- Ask: "If the time is a whole month or year, does English usually prefer 'in' or 'at'?"

## Block-Building Example
- "The keys" + "are" + "on" + "the table."
- "We" + "met" + "at" + "noon."
- "She" + "lives" + "in" + "Singapore."

## Common Pitfalls
- Translating directly from the native language instead of using the English image.
- Mixing "in" and "on" with days and months.
- Treating prepositions like pure rules instead of meaning pictures.
- Guide the student to discover: "Does this feel like a point, a surface, or a container?"
