# Topic: Present Perfect Continuous

## Objective
Teach how English expresses duration from the past until now with "have been" plus an ongoing action.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. Build It Step by Step
- "I have been working," "she has been studying."
- Start with "have been" and then add the "-ing" action.
- This keeps the tense from feeling impossible.
- Ask: "If the action started before now and is still continuing, what helper chunk do we need first?"

### 2. Focus on Duration
- This tense is excellent for ongoing activity over time: "I have been waiting for two hours."
- It answers the feeling of "from then until now."
- Ask: "If I started in the past and I am still doing it now, does English often choose the continuous form?"

### 3. Teach "Since" vs "For"
- "Since" gives the starting point: since Monday, since 2020.
- "For" gives the duration: for two hours, for three years.
- Teach it visually as point vs line.
- Ask: "Is 'three years' a point on the calendar or a length of time?"

## Block-Building Example
- "I" + "have been" + "living" + "here" + "for three years."
- "She" + "has been" + "working" + "since Monday."
- "How long" + "have" + "you been" + "waiting?"

## Common Pitfalls
- Using "since" with a duration: "since three hours."
- Forgetting "been" in the middle of the tense.
- Using this tense with verbs that are usually states, like "know."
- Guide the student to discover: "Are we emphasizing the duration of the activity, or just the finished fact?"
