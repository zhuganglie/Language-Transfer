# Topic: Present Perfect Simple

## Objective
Teach the present perfect as a past action or experience that still matters now.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. Build It with "Have" + Past Participle
- "I have seen," "she has finished," "they have arrived."
- Start with the helper and only then add the participle.
- Keep the logic clear: the action is in the past, but the time window is still open.
- Ask: "If the helper is 'have,' what kind of verb form comes after it?"

### 2. Contrast It with Past Simple
- Past simple: finished past time. "I saw him yesterday."
- Present perfect: no fixed finished time, or life experience. "I have seen him before."
- This contrast is the real lesson, not the form alone.
- Ask: "If you say 'yesterday,' does the time feel closed or still open?"

### 3. Use Common Meaning Patterns
- Experience: "I have been to Japan."
- Recent result: "She has lost her keys."
- Unfinished time period: "I have worked a lot this week."
- Ask: "If the week is not finished yet, which tense often sounds more natural?"

## Block-Building Example
- "I" + "have" + "finished" + "my work."
- "She" + "has" + "never" + "seen" + "snow."
- "Have" + "you" + "ever" + "tried" + "sushi?"

## Common Pitfalls
- Using a finished time marker with the present perfect: "I have seen him yesterday."
- Confusing the past participle with the past simple of irregular verbs.
- Forgetting that "has" is needed for he, she, it.
- Guide the student to notice: "Is the past time finished and named, or is it still connected to now?"
