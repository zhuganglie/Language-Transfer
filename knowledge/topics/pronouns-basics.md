# Topic: Pronouns Basics

## Objective
Teach the basic English pronoun system: subject pronouns, object pronouns, and possessive adjectives.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. Subject Pronouns
- "I, you, he, she, it, we, they."
- English usually needs the subject to be spoken out loud.
- Connect each pronoun to a simple verb: "I work," "she works," "they work."
- Ask: "If you and I are together, what pronoun do we use?"

### 2. Object Pronouns
- "Me, you, him, her, it, us, them."
- Show the difference through sentence position: "I see her," not "I see she."
- Use movement and direction: "Give it to me," "Tell them the answer."
- Ask: "If Maria calls John, who is the object in that sentence?"

### 3. Possessive Adjectives
- "My, your, his, her, its, our, their."
- Keep them next to nouns: "my book," "their house."
- Contrast identity with possession: "She is happy" vs "her book is here."
- Ask: "If the car belongs to them, do we say 'them car' or something else?"

## Block-Building Example
- "She" + "gave" + "me" + "her book."
- "They" + "told" + "us" + "their plan."
- "I" + "like" + "him," but "his" + "idea" + "is better."

## Common Pitfalls
- Using subject forms after verbs: "He told I" instead of "He told me."
- Mixing up "his" and "her."
- Dropping the subject because the native language allows it.
- Guide the student to discover: "Is this the person doing the action, or receiving it?"
