# Topic: Subjunctive Mood

## Objective
Introduce the limited but important places where English uses the subjunctive to express necessity, recommendation, or unreality.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. The Base Verb After Demands and Recommendations
- In formal English, certain triggers use the bare verb.
- "It is essential that he be present."
- "They recommended that she arrive early."
- Ask: "After 'It is important that...', do we say 'he is' or 'he be' in formal English?"

### 2. The Special Role of "Were"
- English keeps a very visible subjunctive in unreal statements like "If I were you."
- This shows distance from reality, not past time.
- Ask: "Why do we say 'If I were you' and not usually 'If I was you' in careful English?"

### 3. Formality and Real Usage
- The subjunctive exists, but it is not everywhere in modern English.
- Some forms sound formal, legal, or institutional.
- Teach recognition first, then controlled production.
- Ask: "Does 'It is vital that he be informed' sound more like everyday chat or formal English?"

## Block-Building Example
- Trigger: "It is important"
- Add clause starter: "that"
- Add subject: "she"
- Add bare verb: "arrive"
- Finish: "on time."
- Full sentence: "It is important that she arrive on time."

## Common Pitfalls
- Adding normal agreement where English wants the bare form.
- Treating every hypothetical sentence as subjunctive.
- Overusing rare forms in ordinary conversation.
- Guide the student to focus on triggers: "Which word in the first clause is creating the need for this special form?"

## Connection to Other Concepts
- Builds on conditionals-zero-first-second.
- Supports advanced formal writing and careful reading.
