# Topic: Third Conditional (Past Regret and Alternative Past)

## Objective
Teach the third conditional as the structure for imagining a different past and its different result.

## Teaching Strategy

### 1. The Core Logic
- We use it for impossible past situations.
- "If I had known, I would have helped."
- One part shows the imagined past condition, and the other shows the imagined result.
- Ask: "Can we still change this past, or are we only imagining it?"

### 2. Build the Two Halves
- "If" clause: had + past participle.
- Result clause: would have + past participle.
- "If she had left earlier, she would have caught the train."
- Ask: "Which side shows the condition, and which side shows the result?"

### 3. Use It for Regret, Blame, and Reflection
- Regret: "If I had studied more, I would have passed."
- Blame: "If you had called me, I would have come."
- Reflection: "If they had listened, things would have been easier."
- Keep the emotional meaning visible, not just the grammar.

## Block-Building Example
- "If I had known"
- "I would have told you"
- Combine: "If I had known, I would have told you."

## Common Pitfalls
- Putting "would have" in the "if" clause.
- Mixing the order of the two halves and losing track of which is the cause.
- Using the wrong participle after "had" or "have."
- Guide the student to discover the time logic: "Which action belongs to the imagined condition, and which belongs to the imagined result?"

## Connection to Other Concepts
- Depends on past-perfect and knowledge of past participles.
- Extends the logic from conditionals-zero-first-second into the impossible past.
